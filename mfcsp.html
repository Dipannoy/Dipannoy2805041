MFCSP — Crystal Structures in a Handful of Steps

Generative crystal structure prediction had stopped being limited by accuracy and started being limited by sampling cost: diffusion and flow-matching samplers integrate a trajectory over tens to thousands of sequential network evaluations for every single candidate. MFCSP learns the average velocity of that trajectory instead of the instantaneous one, so a complete structure can come out of a single evaluation.

The bottleneck was never the model

CDVAE, DiffCSP, MatterGen and CrystalFlow all made it possible to learn the distribution of stable crystals directly instead of searching a potential-energy surface. What they did not fix is inference: every candidate structure costs a full numerical integration of the generative trajectory. DiffCSP needs on the order of 20,000 network evaluations per target; CrystalFlow needs 2,000.

Learning average velocity instead of instantaneous velocity

Standard flow matching learns the instantaneous velocity at a point on the trajectory, which is why it has to be integrated in small steps. MeanFlow learns the average velocity between two times r and t, so a single update can cover a large interval:

x̂r = xt − (t − r) · uθ(xt, t, r)

The field is trained with the MeanFlow self-consistency objective, whose Jacobian–vector products are evaluated with forward-mode automatic differentiation. Classifier-free condition dropout is used during training and the matching guidance formulation during sampling.

Making the Transformer chemistry- and symmetry-aware

The velocity field is a sequence Transformer that treats each atom as a token. Because self-attention is order-sensitive, four design choices carry the chemical and crystallographic priors:

  • Canonical atom ordering. Atoms are sorted by Pauling electronegativity; for symmetry-aware training examples they are additionally grouped into space-group orbits and ordered by Wyckoff letter. This alone gives the largest single jump in space-group match.
  • Per-token chemistry features. A learned atom-type embedding is concatenated with fixed descriptors — electronegativity, atomic radius, period and group — along with the atom's current coordinates and the shared lattice.
  • Global composition embedding. Computed from normalised element counts; contributes the largest structure-match increment.
  • Coarse crystal-system conditioning. A seven-way label rather than a 230-way space group, so each class sees far more training examples. It is replaced by a null token for a fraction of examples, making it an auxiliary training signal, not an inference requirement — at generation time the model runs formula-only.

Design consequence worth stating plainly

Every inference-time conditioning signal is derivable from the composition alone. The space group is never required at generation, which is what makes the model usable on compounds nobody has characterised yet.

What the numbers actually compare

A single MeanFlow step matches CrystalFlow's match rate (78.38% against 78.34%) using 100 times fewer network evaluations and roughly an order of magnitude less wall-clock time. Five steps raise it to 83.64%, above both CrystalFlow (78.34% at 2,000 evaluations) and DiffCSP (77.93% at about 20,000) — still at 20 times fewer evaluations.

The stricter test is CSPBench, which withholds cell size and space group and scores symmetry recovery. MFCSP reaches 72 / 72 / 65% structure, space-group and consensus match at five steps. CrystalFlow scores higher (78 / 73 / 68) at its full 100-step budget but collapses to 49 / 32 / 31% when held to the same five-step budget — less than half the space-group and consensus rates.

The claim being made

MFCSP improves accuracy per network evaluation, not peak accuracy alone. That is the axis that decides whether a generative model can be run over a large candidate space rather than a curated shortlist.

Implementation

A 12-layer Transformer with hidden size 768 and 12 attention heads, trained on the MP-20 subset of the Materials Project using the fixed DiffCSP split, with all evaluation structures removed from training. 700 epochs in mixed bf16 precision with AdamW at a constant learning rate of 10−4 and batch size 256, on a single NVIDIA A100. A companion variant, pMF-CSP, swaps in the latent-free Pixel MeanFlow parameterisation — predicting a denoised state and converting it to an average-velocity estimate — with everything else held identical, to check that the result is not an artefact of one parameterisation.
